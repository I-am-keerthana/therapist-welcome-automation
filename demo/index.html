Automating the new-therapist welcome email

A shared hiring spreadsheet that several people edit is the trigger. The email has to go out once, to the right person, at the right time. If the automation stops working, someone has to find out that day, not weeks later.

The short answer

1

What I would use

Google Apps Script inside the existing hiring sheet, sending from a shared Gmail alias (e.g. onboarding@). The team already works in Google Workspace, so there's no new vendor, login or monthly fee, and the data stays inside your Workspace account.

  • A timed scan every 15 minutes, not an on-edit trigger. On-edit fires while someone is still typing, and it misses pasted or imported rows. A timed scan catches anything a previous run missed.
  • Rows are matched by a Candidate ID, not by row number or name, so sorting and filtering are safe.
  • A 30-minute undo window after someone marks a row "Hired".
  • A protected Welcome Log tab records every send, so no hire is ever emailed twice.

Zapier or Make would also work for the happy path. I chose Apps Script because the hard parts here are idempotency, validation and monitoring, and those need real logic and a place to keep state.

2

What could go wrong

  • Duplicate emails after a re-sort, a re-edit, or a run that dies halfway through a send
  • Sent too early: "Hired" chosen by mistake, or the email typed wrong
  • Column renamed or moved, so the script reads the wrong field
  • Status typos: "Offer Accepted" or "hired " never match
  • Trigger dies when its owner leaves or their access is revoked
  • Gmail quota or outage, or a broken template that sends "Hi {{firstName}}"
  • Old hires backfilled into the sheet all get welcomed at once
  • PHI ending up in an email to a personal inbox
3

How I'd know it silently stopped

A script can't report that it isn't running, so the monitoring has four independent layers:

  1. External heartbeat. Every successful run pings a monitor outside Google (e.g. Healthchecks.io). If the pings stop for 30 minutes, it emails or texts ops.
  2. Loud failures. Any error stops the run, sends nothing, and emails ops. Repeat alerts are suppressed so people don't learn to ignore them.
  3. Outcome check, not just uptime. A daily digest lists every hire starting in the next 14 days who hasn't been welcomed. This catches the quietest failure: the script runs fine, but nobody's row qualifies.
  4. The daily digest always arrives, even when everything is fine. If it stops arriving, that silence is itself the alert.

        Rollout

        1. Day 1. Add a Candidate ID column and the status dropdown. Run setup(). It creates the protected tabs, installs the triggers and sends a test alert.
        2. Week 1, dry run. DRY_RUN=true sends every welcome email to the ops inbox instead of the hire. Compare against the emails still being sent by hand.
        3. Go live. Run clearDryRunEntries() and set DRY_RUN=false. Keep the daily digest.
        4. Hand-over. A runbook covers every alert: what it means, what to check, and how to fix it.

        Questions I'd ask first

        • Who owns the hiring sheet, and which statuses do people actually use today?
        • Should the email wait for signed paperwork or a background check, or go out at "offer accepted"?
        • Does each supervisor or location need its own template, or a CC?
        • Which inbox should replies go to, and who should get the alerts?
        • Does the sheet hold anything beyond HR data? If so, the email pulls only the minimum fields.
